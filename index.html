📚 Data Sources & Assumptions
Where these numbers come from, and what they are (and aren't) certified for.
  • Drag coefficient (Cd) & frontal area — representative mid-range figures for each body class, in line with typical published ranges in vehicle-aerodynamics references (hatchbacks ≈0.30–0.35, sedans ≈0.28–0.36, SUVs/vans ≈0.40–0.50).
  • Rolling resistance (Crr = 0.015) — a standard textbook value for a road tire on dry asphalt (typical range 0.01–0.02).
  • Tire grip & road-surface grip (μ) — standard illustrative ranges used across vehicle-dynamics coursework (dry ≈0.8–0.9, wet ≈0.5–0.6, snow ≈0.2–0.3, ice ≈0.1–0.2).
  • Engine power tiers — rounded, representative values spanning economy to performance cars, not the spec sheet of any single named model.
  • Reaction time (1.5 s) & target stopping zone (60 m) — common assumptions used in driver-education material to illustrate stopping distance, not a measurement from any specific agency.

On accuracy: these are educational approximations for building intuition, not measured, certified, or licensed data from a specific manufacturer, textbook, or company — this project makes no claim of official authorization. For a graded submission, cite the actual textbook or paper your course uses for these constants, or swap in any value your instructor has approved.

Educational model · assumes straight-line motion, a fixed rolling-resistance factor, no gearbox/transmission limits, and (except where noted) a flat road for braking distances.
Real-world results depend on gearing, drivetrain losses, and tire temperature — use this to build intuition, not to certify a design.
